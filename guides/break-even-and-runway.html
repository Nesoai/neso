How to find your break-even point and your runway

Divide your monthly fixed costs by what each sale leaves after its direct costs (the contribution margin). The answer is how many sales a month cover the fixed base. Your runway is how many months your cash lasts while you get there.

How to work it out

  1. Set the average sale. What a typical sale brings in. For a subscription, the monthly price; for hourly work, the rate times the usual hours.
  2. Take off the direct cost. What each sale costs to deliver: materials, ingredients, payment fees. Service businesses often run 5% to 15%; retail and food 25% to 40%.
  3. Add up fixed costs. Rent, payroll, insurance, software, loan payments and the owner's pay: what you spend whether you sell or not.
  4. Divide. Fixed costs ÷ contribution per sale = sales a month to break even.
  5. Check the runway. Starting cash ÷ the monthly shortfall while sales grow = months before the cash runs out.

A worked example

An average sale of $60, a direct cost of 30%, and fixed costs of $9,000 a month.

  • Each sale leaves $60 × (1 − 0.30) = $42.
  • Break-even: $9,000 ÷ $42 ≈ 214 sales a month (rounded up to 215).
  • A common mistake is counting the owner's pay or a loan payment as a cost of each sale. Both are fixed, and leaving them out makes break-even look closer than it is.

What to have ready

  • Your average sale
  • Direct cost as a share of each sale
  • Monthly fixed costs, including your own pay
  • Starting cash
  • Expected monthly growth

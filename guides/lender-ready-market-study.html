What a lender-ready market study includes

A defined trade area, its demographics and growth, where demand comes from, comparable properties with their rents or prices, how fast new space fills (absorption), and a plain conclusion. What makes it lender-ready is that an underwriter can rebuild every number from what is shown.

How to work it out

  1. Draw the trade area. The area your tenants or buyers will come from, named on a map, not "the region".
  2. Show the people. Population, households, income and growth for that area, from the Census and other public sources, with the source named.
  3. Say where demand comes from. Employers, new jobs, people moving in, or a shortage of a kind of space.
  4. List the comparables. Similar properties nearby, with rent or price per unit or square foot and occupancy.
  5. Estimate absorption and conclude. How many units the market takes a month, how long yours will take to fill, and what could go wrong.

A worked example

A 40-unit apartment project in a town where comparable buildings have leased about 3 units a month.

  • At 3 a month, 40 units take about 14 months to lease up (40 ÷ 3, rounded up).
  • The study shows the comparables that pace comes from, so the underwriter can check it.
  • It also names the risks: a competing project in the pipeline, or an employer that might cut jobs.

What to have ready

  • Project name, city or county, and type
  • Size in units or square feet
  • Total development cost
  • Two to five comparable properties
  • The risks you already see

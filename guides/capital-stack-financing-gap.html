How to build a capital stack and find the financing gap

Start from what the project costs, subtract what the bank will lend against its income, then subtract every other source you have. What is left is the gap, and naming it in dollars is the first step to closing it.

How to work it out

  1. Total the development cost. Land, hard costs, soft costs and reserves: every dollar it takes to finish. This is the height the stack has to reach.
  2. Size the senior loan from income. A lender divides the building's net operating income by a coverage ratio (often 1.20 to 1.25) to get the most annual debt service it will accept, then turns that into a loan amount at its rate and term.
  3. Add equity. The sponsor's cash, and any investor equity.
  4. Layer in the rest. Tax credit equity, grants, soft loans from a city or state, and a deferred developer fee, in the order the deal can get them.
  5. Name the gap. Cost minus every source. If it is not zero, the deal needs another source, a lower cost, or more income.

A worked example

A project costs $4,000,000. Its stabilized NOI is $260,000 a year. The sponsor has $400,000 of equity and a $250,000 grant.

  • At a 1.25 coverage ratio, the most annual debt service is $260,000 ÷ 1.25 = $208,000.
  • At 7% over 30 years, each dollar of loan costs about 0.0798 a year, so the loan is $208,000 ÷ 0.0798 ≈ $2,605,331.
  • Sources so far: $2,605,331 + $400,000 + $250,000 = $3,255,331.
  • Gap: $4,000,000 − $3,255,331 ≈ $744,669. That is the amount to find in tax credits, soft loans or a deferred fee.

What to have ready

  • Total development cost
  • Stabilized net operating income, if the building earns rent
  • Equity you have
  • Any grants or soft loans already promised

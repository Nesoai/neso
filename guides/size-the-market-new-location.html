How to size the market for a new location

Build the market from the bottom up: the households (or businesses) in your trade area, times how often they buy, times what they spend. Divide by the number of competitors plus you for a fair share, and compare it with the revenue the location needs.

How to work it out

  1. Draw the trade area. Where your customers will really come from: a 10-minute drive, a neighborhood, a county.
  2. Count the buyers. Households, or businesses if you sell to them, from the Census or a county source.
  3. Estimate how often and how much. Visits or purchases a year, and the spend each time.
  4. Divide by the field. The number of competitors, plus you, gives a fair share before any edge you bring.
  5. Compare with what you need. The revenue the location needs to cover its costs. If your fair share falls short, you need a real edge or a different spot.

A worked example

A café looking at a trade area of 8,000 households, each buying about 12 times a year at $18, with 5 cafés already there and $300,000 a year needed to cover costs.

  • The market: 8,000 × 12 × $18 = $1,728,000 a year.
  • A fair share with 5 competitors plus you: $1,728,000 ÷ 6 = $288,000.
  • That is $12,000 short of the $300,000 needed, so the move works only with a clear edge (location, hours, a product no one else has) or lower costs.

What to have ready

  • What you sell and who buys it
  • The trade area
  • A rough count of competitors
  • The revenue the move needs

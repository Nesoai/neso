How to explain a housing project to an architect, a contractor or a lender

One page that says what the site is, who the homes are for, how many and what kind, what matters most, the budget range, and what you will not do. With that, a professional can tell you in one meeting whether the idea works and what it takes.

How to work it out

  1. Describe the site. Address, lot size and zoning if you know it.
  2. Say who it is for. Workforce, seniors, families, students, or the market.
  3. Give the unit mix. How many studios, one-, two- and three-bedroom units, and how many stories and parking spaces.
  4. Rank what matters. Cost, speed, quality, energy use, design character.
  5. Set the edges. A budget range, must-haves and deal-breakers, and the goal in your own words.

A worked example

A 12-unit workforce-housing building on a half-acre corner lot.

  • Who it is for: teachers, nurses and service workers in the town.
  • Mix: four one-bedroom, six two-bedroom and two three-bedroom units, on three stories, with one parking space per unit.
  • What matters most: cost and energy use. Deal-breaker: no units without a window in every bedroom.
  • On one page, an architect can see in minutes whether the lot fits the program and what to study first.

What to have ready

  • Site and lot size
  • Stage (an idea, a site under contract, or land you own)
  • Who the homes are for
  • Unit mix, stories and parking
  • Budget range, must-haves and deal-breakers

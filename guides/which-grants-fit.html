Which grants fit your organization? A 10-minute check

The grant that fits is the one whose funder already pays for organizations like yours, for the purpose you have, at the size you need. Most rejected applications fail on fit, not on writing.

How to work it out

  1. Name what you are. A nonprofit, a for-profit small business, a town or county, or a tribe. Many grants are open to only one kind, so this alone rules out most of the list.
  2. Name what the money is for. Operating costs, equipment, a building, research, training or a specific program. Funders pay for purposes, not for organizations in general.
  3. Size the ask. A $5,000 need and a $500,000 need go to different funders. A small foundation rarely funds a building; a federal program rarely bothers with a small equipment buy.
  4. Be honest about your stage. A new organization with no audited financials is a long shot for most federal grants and a fair shot for many local foundations.
  5. Look at place and certifications. Rural, distressed and Delta areas, and certifications such as minority-, women- or veteran-owned, open set-aside programs.

A worked example

A two-year-old nonprofit wants $40,000 for a youth job-training program in a rural county.

  • Federal grants: possible, but the first federal award usually goes to organizations with audited financials and a track record.
  • Local and regional foundations: a strong fit. Job training for young people in a rural county is what many community foundations fund.
  • Corporate giving: a fair fit if a local employer hires the trainees.
  • SBA and CDFI lending: not a fit. This is program money, not something to borrow against.

What to have ready

  • What kind of organization you are and how long you have operated
  • The purpose, in one sentence
  • The amount
  • Your county and state
  • Any certifications you hold

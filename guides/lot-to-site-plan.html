From a lot to a site plan: setbacks and buildable area

Take the lot's dimensions, subtract the front, rear and side setbacks your zoning requires, and what is left is the buildable envelope. Height limits, lot coverage and parking rules can shrink it further.

How to work it out

  1. Measure the lot. Width along the street and depth back from it, in feet.
  2. Find the setbacks. Your zoning code lists the front, rear and side setbacks for the district. Corner lots often have two fronts.
  3. Subtract them. Width minus both side setbacks; depth minus front and rear.
  4. Check the other limits. Maximum lot coverage, floor area ratio, height and parking can each cap what you build.
  5. Sketch the building in the envelope. Footprint, stories and parking, before anyone draws it for real.

A worked example

A 60 × 140 ft lot, with a 25 ft front setback, a 20 ft rear setback and 5 ft on each side.

  • Buildable width: 60 − 5 − 5 = 50 ft. Buildable depth: 140 − 25 − 20 = 95 ft.
  • Buildable footprint: 50 × 95 = 4,750 square feet, out of a 8,400 square-foot lot.
  • Two stories on that footprint is up to 9,500 square feet, if lot coverage, floor area and parking rules allow it.

What to have ready

  • The lot's width and depth
  • The district's setbacks from your zoning code
  • What you want to build: homes, a row of units, or a building with a unit mix
  • Stories and parking

Mallory Bailey, MBA
Principal · business and technology

One of us has been in real-estate development since 2005, alongside contractors, engineers and architects, on jobs large and small — and built the software NESO runs on.

The other has spent seventeen years in land use, entitlements and development capital. Ten thousand permits processed. A hundred million dollars in incentives secured. Four hundred and eighty-four megawatts of energy projects sited. An MPA, and years inside city, county and regional agencies before any of this. He reads and approves every land report before it goes out — a rule we wrote down, not a habit. We introduce him by name when a project needs him, which is most of the time.

That is the whole firm. Write in and one of us writes back within a business day. No account manager, no queue, no ticket number.

We started NESO in 2026 for a plain reason: we kept watching people commit real money before anybody had answered the one small question that decided it. So we built eight tools that answer those questions free, in your browser, with no account — put every price in public next to the thing it belongs to — and made sure a person’s judgement sits behind everything that leaves. These days that also means building websites for twenty dollars a month, because a business nobody can find has the same problem in a different shape.

NESO is New England and Southern. New England and the areas around it, and the Southern states — the two parts of the country we know the ground in. The work itself goes anywhere in the United States.

The ones people ask first

What does free really mean?

You get the whole answer, using your own numbers. No account, no email, and nothing runs out.

Paying adds the printed copy and more depth. It never adds the answer.

What does it cost?

The tools are free. A land report has one price. Other work is quoted in writing before it starts.

Every price is on the website, next to the thing it belongs to. We do not quote from memory and we do not discount privately.

Who checks the work?

The reviewing principal reads and approves every land report before it leaves. Nothing goes out on its own.

Where do you work?

Anywhere in the United States. The tools have no geography in them at all — they run on your numbers wherever you are.

On a land report, where the parcel is changes how much of the answer comes from the record and how much comes from knowing the place. We say which is which in the report, and we say it before you pay.

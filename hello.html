Groundwork for what you build
Why it exists

The order these things usually happen in is backwards.

A developer signs a purchase agreement and then finds out the parcel is not entitled for what they intended. A founder writes a thirty-page business plan before checking whether the model clears break-even. A municipality lets a funding deadline pass because nobody on staff had two weeks to write the application.

None of those are hard questions. They are questions that arrive late, because the people who can answer them bill by the hour — so the cheap early question costs a retainer conversation, and people skip it and pay for it later.

The gap

Between a spreadsheet template and a ten-thousand-dollar consulting engagement there is almost nothing. That gap is the whole business.

What software misses

A calculator returns a number. What somebody actually needs is a person with standing saying whether that number means go or stop.

So the tools are free

Every tool shows the complete analysis on your own numbers. Paying adds the printable exhibit, the editing and the depth — never the insight.

How the work goes

Six things that are true of every engagement.

  • A quote comes first. Anything scoped is quoted in writing before it starts. A payment link is not a quote.
  • The clock starts at scope, not at checkout. We ask for three things — the parcel or address, the jurisdiction, and the decision you are trying to make — and the promised date is set when the last one arrives.
  • Pauses are day for day. If we are waiting on you the clock stops, and a pause is never counted as a penalty day.
  • A principal releases it. Nothing ships without senior review. That caps how much NESO can take on, and the cap is the point.
  • Rush is capped firm-wide. Two slots a week across the whole firm. When the week is full the option is refused rather than queued — that cap is what makes the promise real.
  • Sources are named. Research comes from zoning codes, GIS layers, overlays and public records, cited so you can check them yourself.

Prices are published rather than negotiated privately, because a private discount becomes the price everyone hears about. Refund terms are stated on each listing.
